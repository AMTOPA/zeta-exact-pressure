# Sixth-moment compatibility audit

Status: research design note.  No theorem beyond `19/27` is claimed.

This note asks a precise question: can modern sixth-moment / unbalanced-sum technology be adapted to the large-modulus term in Bui--Heath-Brown strongly enough to permit a mollifier length `theta>1/2`?

Primary references:

- Bui--Heath-Brown, arXiv:1302.5018.
- Conrey--Iwaniec--Soundararajan, arXiv:0710.5176.
- Chandee--Li--Matomäki--Radziwill, arXiv:2409.01457.

## 1. The exact Bui--Heath-Brown family

After dyadic dissection, their hard term has

\[
Q>\eta,\qquad D\le K,\qquad KQ\ll y=T^\theta,
\]

and total Dirichlet length

\[
X=\frac{KQT}{\pi D}.
\]

After the generalized Vaughan identity and Perron, they must control

\[
T(Q,V)=
\sum_{q\asymp Q}\sum_{\psi\bmod q}^{*}
\int_{-V}^{V}|H_1(\psi,t)\cdots H_9(\psi,t)|\,dt,
\]

where the factors have divisor/log/Möbius-type coefficients and total length `X`.

The current proof takes absolute values, splits the nine factors into two blocks, and applies Cauchy plus the hybrid large sieve.

## 2. Why the modern theorems are not black-box replacements

| feature | Bui--HB hard family | CIS 2007 | CLMR 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| modulus average | dyadic `q~Q`, with outer `K,D` weights | smooth `q~Q` | smooth `q~Q` |
| characters | primitive | primitive even (odd analogous) | primitive even (odd analogous) |
| `t` variable | artificial Perron integral, dyadic `1<=V<=T^5` | short completed-L critical-line average | central point, no `t` average |
| coefficients | nine-factor Vaughan decomposition; logs, Möbius pieces, `d`-dependence | coefficients generated by three shifted L-factors | shifted ternary-divisor coefficients from three L-factors |
| natural total length | `X=KQT/D` | conductor-sixth-moment scale | `MN<=Q^3` after approximate functional equation |
| unbalanced ranges | disposed of by Pólya--Vinogradov / generic two-block large sieve | short `t` average suppresses the hardest imbalance | handled explicitly by complementary divisor, functional equation, Poisson/Kuznetsov and spectral large sieve |

Therefore neither theorem can simply be cited to replace Bui--HB Lemma 2.  The relevant object is an **adaptation of the machinery**, or a new twisted/mollified sixth-moment theorem matching the Bui weights.

## 3. Optimize the old long-factor threshold before importing new technology

Bui--HB fix the long-factor cutoff at

\[
R=yT^{1/2}.
\]

For `theta<1/2` this is sufficient, but for crossing the endpoint it is better to keep `Q,K,D` and optimize `R` dyadically.

If all factors longer than `R` are disposed of by the same Pólya--Vinogradov argument, the two competing contributions have the shape

\[
\frac{Q^2T}{R}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\sqrt{\frac DK}\,T^{1/2}R^{1/2}.
\]

Balancing gives

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm opt}=Q^{4/3}T^{1/3}\left(\frac KD\right)^{1/3}.
}
\]

Write

\[
Q=T^\alpha,\qquad K=T^\kappa,\qquad D=T^\delta,
\qquad g=\kappa-\delta\ge0.
\]

The balanced long-factor / `R`-branch exponent is

\[
\frac{2\alpha}{3}+\frac23-\frac g3,
\]

and the old `2/3` factorization branch has exponent

\[
\frac56+\frac\alpha3-\frac g6.
\]

Both are power-saved exactly when

\[
\boxed{g>2\alpha-1}.
\]

Hence the only unresolved power range has

\[
\boxed{
\alpha\ge\frac12,\qquad 0\le g\le2\alpha-1.
}
\]

## 4. The critical region equals the sixth-moment conductor region

Since

\[
X=\frac{KQT}{D}=T^{1+\alpha+g},
\]

the condition `g<=2alpha-1` is exactly

\[
1+\alpha+g\le3\alpha,
\]
that is

\[
\boxed{X\le Q^3}.
\]

This is the main structural observation of the audit:

> After optimizing the old cutoff, the only blocks not already power-saved by the Bui--Heath-Brown mechanism are precisely the blocks lying at or below the natural sixth-moment conductor length `Q^3`.

The apparently mismatched modern sixth-moment technology is therefore aligned with exactly the remaining corner, not with the entire original parameter space.

For a 71% target,

\[
\theta_{71}=0.5107780534895\ldots.
\]

Thus the critical moduli satisfy only

\[
T^{1/2}\lesssim Q\lesssim T^{0.510778\ldots},
\]

and, because `KQ<=T^theta`, the outer `K`-range in the top corner is extremely short.  At `Q~y`, one has essentially `K,D=T^{o(1)}` and `X~QT<Q^3`.

## 5. Quantitative power-saving target

At the worst corner `alpha=theta`, `g=0`, the optimized old estimate exceeds `T^1` by

\[
T^{(2\theta-1)/3}.
\]

If an adapted signed/asymptotic family estimate supplies a net modulus saving `Q^{-sigma}`, the benchmark condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sigma>\frac{2\theta-1}{3\theta}.
}
\]

Representative milestones:

| theta | formal simple-zero proportion | required sigma in worst corner |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.502 | 70.48857354% | 0.00265604 |
| 0.505 | 70.66470228% | 0.00660066 |
| 0.506 | 70.72310033% | 0.00790514 |
| 0.5069 | 70.77552612% | 0.00907477 |
| 0.5107780535 | 71.00000000% | 0.01406750 |

CLMR 2024 prove their untwisted shifted sixth-moment asymptotic with an error `O(Q^(2-11/1196+eps))`, so their published saving is

\[
\frac{11}{1196}=0.0091973244\ldots.
\]

This **does not automatically transfer** to the Bui family.  As a scale comparison only, an unchanged saving of that size would correspond to roughly

\[
\theta\approx0.50699449,
\qquad
\kappa^*_{\rm formal}\approx70.7810\%.
\]

CIS 2007 have a much larger nominal power saving (`Q^(19/10+eps)` versus a `Q^2` main scale), but their short completed-L `t` average is an essential part of the theorem.  The obstacle there is compatibility, not numerical saving.

## 6. A better first milestone than 71%

The first rigorous target should be

\[
\boxed{\theta=0.502},
\]

which already gives the formal one-piece proportion

\[
\boxed{70.48857354\%}.
\]

It needs only a very small `Q^{-0.00266}`-scale net saving in the worst critical block.  Proving any fixed `theta>1/2` would be the structural breakthrough; optimizing theta can come afterwards.

## 7. What a successful new lemma must preserve

A usable theorem must address, rather than hide, the following features:

1. a primitive-character family with `Q>=T^(1/2)` in the critical corner;
2. total Dirichlet length `X<=Q^3`;
3. short extra parameters `K,D` with `D|K`-type arithmetic structure;
4. logarithmic and short Möbius factors from the generalized Vaughan identity;
5. the signed Gauss-sum / character weights occurring before Bui--HB take absolute values;
6. either removal of the Perron `t` variable or uniform control of the relevant dyadic `V` ranges;
7. a power-saving error, not merely an upper bound of the correct order.

The last point matters: a positive sixth-moment upper bound at the natural family scale does not by itself cross `theta=1/2`.  The new argument must retain enough signed/off-diagonal cancellation to gain a power in the critical corner.

## 8. Current research choice

The most promising route is to isolate the critical `X<=Q^3` blocks and adapt the **unbalanced-sum spectral machinery** of CLMR 2024 there, while leaving `X>Q^3` to the optimized old Bui--HB argument.

A parallel route is to return one step earlier to the original additive sum

\[
\sum_{k\le y}\frac{b(k)}k
\sum_{m\le kT/(2\pi)}a_\nu(m)e(-m/k)
\]

and exploit additive/Voronoi structure before converting to multiplicative characters.  This is attractive because the critical width above `theta=1/2` is governed by the small exponent `2theta-1`, but it requires a suitable transformation theory for the actual `a_nu` coefficients rather than for a toy `d_3` sequence.
